## Which seasonal topics (Oct 31-Nov 2) have high search volume and a surprising real-history angle?

### Takeaway
Four seasonal angles have verifiable surprises: the Canción Criolla day was created by a 1944 decree and timed to the Señor de los Milagros, not Halloween; Mexico City's Día de Muertos parade began in 2016 after the James Bond film *Spectre*; La Catrina started as a satire of social climbers; and Lima's Nueva Esperanza cemetery draws about 2 million people on Nov 1.

### Cited Findings
- Canción Criolla: a supreme resolution of 18 October 1944 by President Manuel Prado Ugarteche (with Education Minister Pedro M. Oliveira, promoted by El Comercio director Carlos A. Saco) set every Oct 31 as the day. The date was chosen to coincide with the Señor de los Milagros festivity. On the first celebration, flags flew in Lima and Callao and a mass was held for deceased criollo musicians. Singer Lucha Reyes later died on that date (Wikipedia calls it a coincidence). The day is described as a political recognition of criollo culture in reaction to indigenismo and Andean migration to Lima — [Wikipedia ES](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%ADa_de_la_Canci%C3%B3n_Criolla)
- In 2026, Oct 31 falls on a Saturday — [Hiraoka](https://hiraoka.com.pe/blog/post/dia-de-la-cancion-criolla-cuando-es-y-por-que-se-celebra); the music blends Spanish, African and Indigenous influences — [Inkaterra](https://www.inkaterra.com/blog/the-day-of-creole-song/)
- Día de Muertos parade (CDMX): the first government parade was on Oct 29, 2016 under mayor Miguel Ángel Mancera, along Reforma to the Zócalo, widely traced to the opening of *Spectre* (2015), which was filmed in CDMX. Some outlets say the film inspired the format, not the tradition; a 2014 "Mega Procesión de Catrinas" already existed, and in 2024 mayor Clara Brugada credited that citizen procession — [México Desconocido](https://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/desfiles-dia-de-muertos-james-bond.html); [Xataka MX](https://www.xataka.com.mx/cine-y-tv/curiosa-historia-como-superproduccion-300-millones-dolares-cambio-para-siempre-dia-muertos-mexico); [Grupo Animal](https://grupoanimal.mx/estilo-de-vida/desfile-dia-muertos-cdmx-origen); [VidaExtra](https://www.vidaextra.com/cine/desfile-dia-muertos-spectre-nunca-habia-existido-ciudad-mexico-copio-pelicula-james-bond)
- La Catrina: José Guadalupe Posada's zinc etching (about 1910-1913) was originally "La Calavera Garbancera", mocking people of Indigenous origin who wanted to look European. Diego Rivera dressed her up and renamed her "Catrina" (from *catrín*, a dandy) in the mural "Sueño de una tarde dominical en la Alameda Central" (1947 or 1948; sources differ) — [Milenio](https://www.milenio.com/cultura/catrina-significado-origen); [UnoTV](https://www.unotv.com/nacional/dia-de-muertos-2020-cual-es-el-origen-de-la-catrina-y-por-que-se-llama-asi/); [Prensa Libre](https://www.prensalibre.com/vida/que-es-la-catrina-y-cual-es-su-historia/)
- Nueva Esperanza (Virgen de Lourdes) cemetery, Villa María del Triunfo, Lima: 60+ hectares, more than 1 million niches, about 2 million visitors every Nov 1, with music, food and dancing at the graves. It is called the largest in Latin America/South America and second in the world after Wadi Al-Salam (Iraq); the rankings vary by source — [La República](https://larepublica.pe/mundo/2024/10/27/este-cementerio-en-latinoamerica-es-mas-extenso-que-el-coliseo-romano-y-cada-1-noviembre-reune-cerca-de-dos-millones-de-personas-cementerio-de-nueva-esperanza-villa-maria-del-triunfo-lima-peru-evat-2581848); [Andina](https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-dia-todos-los-santos-cementerio-mas-grande-sudamerica-esta-lima-fotos-960683.aspx); [Expreso](https://www.expreso.com.pe/actualidad/dia-de-todos-los-santos-miles-de-peruanos-llenaron-cementerios-con-musica-y-flores-para-recordar-a-sus-seres-queridos-dia-de-los-muertos-1-de-noviembre-feriado-vmt-nueva-esperanza-noticia/1146568/)

### Inferences
- "Halloween vs Canción Criolla" is a recurring Peruvian debate every Oct 31; the fact that the date was chosen for the Señor de los Milagros (not as an anti-Halloween move) is a ready-made myth-busting gag.
- The Bond-parade story crosses borders (Mexico plus the rest of LatAm plus Spain) and has a built-in "life imitates movies" punchline.

## Which "weird real history" themes (bizarre, macabre, absurd) fit the season?

### Takeaway
The best macabre-but-safe angle is the Inca "month of the dead" (Aya Marcay Quilla, November), when mummified ancestors were carried on litters, fed, given drinks and dressed. The Catrina "garbancera" satire and the Bond-copied parade are also strong absurd-history picks.

### Cited Findings
- In Guamán Poma's chronicle, November is "Aia Marcai Quilla", the month of the dead: the dead were carried through the streets on litters, fed, given drinks and dressed while mourners sang and danced ("la fiesta de los defuntos") — [Fulcrum](https://www.fulcrum.org/concern/file_sets/9c67wq66k?locale=en); [Guamán Poma edition TOC, Univ. of Texas Press](https://utpress.utexas.edu/9781477323410/)
- Guamán Poma sent his illustrated manuscript to the King of Spain (1615/1616; sources differ) and wanted him to see the mummified forms of Inca ancestors — [Oxford Academic, Empires of the Dead](https://academic.oup.com/book/46629/chapter/410059316)

### Inferences
- Peru has both the Inca mummy parade (Nov) and the modern cemetery party (Nov 1), so a "Peruvians have partied with their dead for 500+ years" Short connects both with verifiable facts.

### Gaps
- The Royal Danish Library hosts a digitized Guamán Poma manuscript, but no URL for the specific November drawing was retrieved; get it before using the image or exact folio.
